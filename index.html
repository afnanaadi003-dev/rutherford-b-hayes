Reconciliation & Executive Integrity (1877–1881)

In-depth study of the 1876 election compromise, civil service reform, and monetary policy

I. The Contested Election of 1876 & The Compromise of 1877

The presidential election of 1876 between Republican Rutherford B. Hayes and Democrat Samuel J. Tilden remains the most fiercely contested in U.S. history. With 20 disputed electoral votes in South Carolina, Florida, Louisiana, and Oregon, Congress established a 15-member Electoral Commission. The commission awarded the votes to Hayes by an 8-to-7 party-line vote.

Through the informal Compromise of 1877, Hayes agreed to withdraw federal occupation troops from remaining Southern statehouses, effectively concluding military Reconstruction while promising federal protection for constitutional rights.

II. Executive Independence & Civil Service Reform

Fulfilling his pledge to restore executive integrity after Grant-era scandals, Hayes attacked political party assessments and spoils machine hiring. He issued executive orders forbidding federal civil servants from being managed as political machine workers and defied Senator Roscoe Conkling by removing Chester A. Arthur from the Collector of the Port of New York.

III. Monetary Stabilization & Legal Milestones

Hayes championed sound fiscal governance by implementing the Specie Resumption Act of 1875 on January 1, 1879, backing paper greenbacks with gold reserves. Though he vetoed the inflationary Bland-Allison Silver Act, Congress overrode his veto. In 1879, President Hayes signed a historic bill enabling women attorneys (led by Belva Lockwood) to practice before the United States Supreme Court.

IV. Foreign Relations & Great Western Tour of 1880

Hayes became the first sitting President to travel to the Pacific Coast during his 71-day Great Western Tour of 1880, promoting post-war national unity across 10,000 miles. In foreign policy, he asserted U.S. jurisdiction over interoceanic canal routes and arbitrated the post-War of the Triple Alliance boundary between Paraguay and Argentina (earning Paraguay's President Hayes Department honor).

First Lady & Reformer

Lucy Webb Hayes: Pioneer & First Lady

Lucy Webb Hayes was the first First Lady to hold a college degree (Wesleyan Female College, 1850). Known as "Lemonade Lucy" for banning alcohol in the White House during the temperance era, she originated the official White House Easter Egg Roll in 1878 and advocated for wounded veterans, orphans, and prison reform.
